Leads into estimates

How do you cut no-shows on tree estimates?

By Zachary Spencer, founder of Zenith Co.
Published October 5, 2026

Short answer

Book a specific time in the first conversation, confirm it in writing with the address and the job, send a reminder the day before that they can reply to, make moving the time easy, and text when you're on the way. Most homeowners who miss an estimate forgot or had their day change. They weren't avoiding you.

A no-show costs more than it looks. It's the drive out, the drive back, and the slot on the calendar that could have gone to someone who was home. For a company where the owner does the estimates, it's an hour or two of the most expensive time in the business. Most of them are preventable, and most of the prevention happens before the visit, not at it.

Why homeowners miss estimates

  • They forgot. They booked on Monday for Thursday, and nothing reminded them.
  • The time was vague. "Thursday morning" means 8 AM to one person and 11:30 to another.
  • Their day changed and moving the time felt like more trouble than skipping it.
  • They booked someone else who answered faster, and didn't think to cancel.
  • They never really booked. "Swing by sometime this week" isn't an appointment.

Every one of those has a fix, and none of them needs a hard-sell script.

1. Book a firm time in the first conversation

A time the homeowner picks is a time they own. Offer two specific slots ("Thursday at 9 or Friday at 2") and let them choose. A time they chose is a commitment. "We'll call you to set something up" gives them a reason to drift.

Speed matters here too. The homeowner who books within minutes of asking is the one least likely to have also booked a competitor. Why the first few minutes decide it.

2. Confirm it in writing

Right after booking, send a text with the day, the time, the address and what you're coming to look at. Something like:

You're booked with Ridge Tree for Thursday at 9:00 AM at 412 Palm Ave to look at the oak over the driveway. Reply here if anything changes.

That one text fixes three problems: they have the time in writing, you've caught a wrong address before you drive to it, and they know they can reply to the same thread.

3. Remind them the day before

Send a reminder the afternoon or evening before, with the time and an easy way to move it. Ask for a reply. "Reply YES to confirm or tell us a better time" turns a passive reminder into a check. A homeowner who replies has told you they're coming. One who doesn't is worth a quick call before you plan the morning around them.

4. Make moving the time easy

A moved estimate isn't a lost estimate. A homeowner who can't make Thursday and has to call your office during business hours to say so will often just not be there. One who can reply "can we do Friday instead?" to the reminder will move it. Treat a reschedule as a win.

5. Text when you're on the way

"On our way, about 20 minutes out" does two things. It catches the homeowner who forgot before you've driven the whole way, and it makes you look like the organized company, which matters when they're comparing quotes.

When they still aren't there

Knock, wait a few minutes, text and call. If there's no answer, leave a short text that you came by and how to rebook, then move on. Log it as a no-show. If the same source of leads produces a lot of no-shows, that tells you something about the source or about how those leads are being booked.

Who should pay for a no-show

If someone else is booking your estimates, the no-show risk should sit with them, not you. That's how we set up our billing: estimates where the homeowner wasn't there don't bill. You mark each one shown or not shown within 3 business days, and only shown estimates carry our fee. It keeps everyone's attention on the same thing, which is a homeowner standing in the yard. Questions about no-shows

Why do homeowners miss tree estimate appointments?

Mostly because they forgot, the time was vague, their day changed and moving it was a hassle, or they booked a company that answered faster and didn't cancel. Very few are avoiding you on purpose, which is why reminders and easy rescheduling work.

When should you send a reminder for a tree estimate?

The afternoon or evening before, with the time, the address and an easy way to move it. Ask for a reply to confirm. Then text when you're on the way the day of the visit.

What should an estimate confirmation text say?

Your company name, the day and time, the address, what you're coming to look at, and that they can reply to change it. For example: you're booked with us Thursday at 9:00 AM at 412 Palm Ave to look at the oak over the driveway, reply here if anything changes.

Should I charge homeowners for missed estimates?

Most tree companies don't, because free estimates are expected and a fee scares off the people who would have shown up. It's usually better to prevent no-shows with a firm time, a written confirmation and a reminder they can reply to.

Do you pay Zenith Co. for estimates where nobody was home?

No. You mark each estimate shown or not shown within 3 business days, and only estimates where the homeowner was there to walk the job carry our fee.
